# ApplyPilot: 10 launch-preview reel scripts

Status: scripts and production briefs. Ten separate silent caption-card MP4 drafts have also been rendered (25 seconds, 1080×1920); they do not implement the full voiceover/footage directions below. No posts scheduled. English for a global audience. Intended length: 20–30 seconds each, portrait 1080×1920. No claim of virality or guaranteed hiring results.

Use only the clearly labelled fictional example on mybillflow.online/demo.html for footage. Do not use applicant CVs, real inboxes, Stripe records or unlicensed music. Keep a small “Fictional example” label visible wherever the candidate is shown. Captions in the central safe area, maximum two lines at once. End on “Explore the example · mybillflow.online”. Personalised paid preparation is not open: do not say “buy now”, “get your AI preview now” or show a fake purchase/customer success story. Price, if shown: “Planned application pack · $9 USD once · not open for purchase yet”.

## 1. The job title is not the evidence
**Hook, 0–3s:** “Your job title tells me where you sat. What did you actually do?”
**Voiceover, 3–22s:** “Operations Coordinator is a title. Confirming delivery dates, updating schedules and preparing Excel reports—that is the work. Put those responsibilities where a reader can see them. Keep the facts. Make the connection to the role clear.”
**Shots:** Role title alone → highlight three responsibilities in the fictional source → reveal the tailored experience section.
**On-screen beats:** “Title ≠ evidence” / “What you did” / “Why it matters for this role”.
**CTA, 22–27s:** “See the complete fictional application example.”
**Caption:** A clearer application starts with work you can explain. Explore the example, including the original CV.

## 2. A better bullet does not need a fake number
**Hook:** “Please stop inventing a percentage to improve your CV.”
**Voiceover:** “If your records do not support ‘improved efficiency by thirty percent’, leave it out. You can still be specific. What did you collect? Which tool did you use? What did you produce? Delivery updates. Excel. Monthly progress reports. That is a useful sentence without a fictional achievement.”
**Shots:** Cross out an explicitly labelled bad example “Improved efficiency 30%” → three chips “Inputs / Tool / Output” → actual demo report bullet.
**On-screen beats:** “Specific beats made-up” / “Inputs + tool + output” / “Only claim what happened”.
**CTA:** “Explore an example built from supplied facts.”
**Caption:** Strong wording should never create new experience. Fictional demonstration, not a customer result.

## 3. Before you pay, show me the work
**Hook:** “Before I spend nine dollars, show me what changes.”
**Voiceover:** “Here is the original bullet. Here is the clearer version. And here is the requirement it connects to. You should be able to inspect the reasoning before deciding whether an application pack helps you.”
**Shots:** Original reporting sentence → revised sentence → explanation card → job requirement quote. Hold each long enough to read.
**On-screen beats:** “Before” / “After” / “Why this edit?”
**CTA:** “The full fictional example is open to explore.”
**Caption:** Useful proof first. Our planned $9 one-time pack is not open for purchase yet; inspect the example today.

## 4. Not in your CV does not mean you cannot do it
**Hook:** “Your CV does not mention SAP. That does not mean you have never used SAP.”
**Voiceover:** “Missing evidence and missing experience are different things. If you have used the tool, add the real task and context. If you have not, do not claim it. An application assistant should help you check the gap, not write your history for you.”
**Shots:** Posting says “SAP preferred” → source skills → action-plan card, highlight “Check whether you have actually used SAP”.
**On-screen beats:** “Not evidenced ≠ not experienced” / “Verify” / “Add only what is true”.
**CTA:** “See the requirement action plan in the example.”
**Caption:** Do not let an AI guess your experience. Review every statement before applying.

## 5. Your cover letter should have receipts
**Hook:** “Could this cover letter belong to literally anyone?”
**Voiceover:** “If it only says ‘passionate, motivated and hardworking’, it tells the employer very little about your work. Start with one requirement. Connect it to a responsibility you have actually held. Then explain another relevant piece of experience. Make the letter belong to this application.”
**Shots:** Generic adjectives fade → job requirement → supplier scheduling evidence → corresponding letter paragraph.
**On-screen beats:** “Requirement” / “Your evidence” / “A letter with a point”.
**CTA:** “Read and download the fictional letter example.”
**Caption:** A useful letter connects the role with your actual work. No invented qualifications or results.

## 6. Three questions to ask your own CV
**Hook:** “Read one CV bullet. Can you answer these three questions?”
**Voiceover:** “What did I do? What information or tool did I work with? What was the output? You do not need to force every bullet into the same formula. But if a sentence says almost nothing, these questions give you somewhere useful to start.”
**Shots:** Three large question cards → highlight answers within the fictional reporting experience → final bullet.
**On-screen beats:** “Action?” / “Tool or input?” / “Output?”
**CTA:** “See a worked example, not just another checklist.”
**Caption:** An editing prompt you can use yourself. The example shows the source, edit and reason together.

## 7. An interview answer is not a fictional success story
**Hook:** “AI should not invent the story you tell in an interview.”
**Voiceover:** “Start with a responsibility from your own CV. Recall one real occasion. Explain what you personally did and the outcome you can stand behind. If you cannot remember a metric, do not manufacture one. Use preparation to organise your memory, not replace it.”
**Shots:** Interview question → source evidence → “Situation / Your action / What happened” prompts → no invented performance chart.
**On-screen beats:** “Your facts” / “Your actions” / “Your actual outcome”.
**CTA:** “Explore the evidence-based interview workspace.”
**Caption:** Starting answers and prompts for truthful specifics. No fabricated interview stories or hiring promises.

## 8. The CV and letter should sound like the same person
**Hook:** “Your CV says ‘supported’. Your letter says ‘led’. Which one is true?”
**Voiceover:** “Tailoring an application should not upgrade your responsibility level. If you supported a project, explain that contribution clearly. Keep the employer, dates and scope consistent across the CV and letter. A stronger application still needs to be your application.”
**Shots:** Side-by-side fictional bad example “supported / led” → red correction → consistent CV and letter responsibilities.
**On-screen beats:** “Same facts” / “Same responsibility level” / “One coherent application”.
**CTA:** “Compare the CV and letter in the full example.”
**Caption:** Better wording should preserve the work you actually did. Fictional demonstration.

## 9. What would nine dollars need to include?
**Hook:** “A score on a screen is not an application.”
**Voiceover:** “A useful pack needs documents you can use and guidance you can act on. A tailored CV. A specific cover letter. Interview starting answers. A requirement action plan. And an explanation of what changed. Inspect those deliverables before you decide whether they are worth paying for.”
**Shots:** Five deliverable cards → actual Word/PDF controls → before/after explanation. Avoid fake scores or testimonials.
**On-screen beats:** “CV + letter” / “Interview preparation” / “Specific next steps” / “Explain the edits”.
**CTA:** “Explore the full example. Planned price: nine dollars once.”
**Caption:** Preview-stage product. The planned $9 USD pack is not yet open for purchase. Free fictional example available now.

## 10. The last thirty seconds before you apply
**Hook:** “Before you press submit, check these details yourself.”
**Voiceover:** “Your name and contact details. Employer names and dates. Every tool or qualification mentioned. The job title in your letter. Then open the exported file and read it—not just the website preview. AI can help draft an application. You still need to own every sentence.”
**Shots:** Fictional contact fields → employer/date line → skills → letter target → actual exported CV page.
**On-screen beats:** “Contacts” / “Dates and employers” / “Claims” / “The actual file”.
**CTA:** “Use the example to see the complete application pack.”
**Caption:** A practical final review. ApplyPilot provides digital CV assistance, not recruitment or guaranteed employment.

## Production and publishing checks

- Make these distinct videos: separate opening typography and first-frame visual for each hook, not ten identical clips with a changed title.
- Voiceover at a natural pace; edit script to fit 20–30 seconds without rushing. Burn in accurate captions and proofread on a phone.
- Music optional; use original audio or a platform-cleared track at publishing time. No third-party logos suggesting employer endorsement.
- Human review before publication: factual claims, visible fictional-example label, current feature availability and working CTA.
- No scheduling has been performed. ApplyPilot social profiles and the intended Metricool brand still need to be identified before publishing.
